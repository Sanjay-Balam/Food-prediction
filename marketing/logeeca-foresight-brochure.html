The problem

Every kitchen runs on a guess.

Every night, someone decides how much biryani to cook, how much mutton to order and how many naans to prep — from memory and yesterday’s numbers. Guess high and good food is thrown away. Guess low and guests hear “sold out”.

290 million tonnes of food were wasted by restaurants and other food service worldwide in 2022. Source: UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024
01

Prep by guesswork

Weekends, festivals, Ramzan evenings and rainy days move demand far more than memory can track, so every day’s prep is a gamble.

02

Stock surprises

Ingredients are ordered on instinct. Mutton runs out on Sunday while vegetables and dairy spoil by Tuesday.

03

Invisible waste

Leftovers go in the bin without a record, so nobody knows which dishes are over-prepared or what that costs each month.

04

Problems found too late

You learn a bestseller has sold out when a guest asks for it — not the night before, when you could still act.

05

Revenue left on the table

Sold-out bestsellers, slow dishes and quiet weekdays cap your takings without anyone noticing.

Demand Forecasting
